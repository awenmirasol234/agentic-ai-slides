PRESENTATION PLAN: AGENTIC AI — THE EVOLUTION OF AUTONOMOUS AI SYSTEMS

9 slides | Text-only presentation | Beginner-friendly

PRESENTATION OBJECTIVE Explain how AI evolved from passive chatbots into
persistent autonomous AI systems, how modern autonomous agents operate,
and how real-world platforms—including Hermes Agent, OpenClaw, Muse,
Grok Bot, Manus, OpenAI Operator, and Claude Computer Use—are shaping
personal, team, and enterprise workflows.

SLIDE 1 — INTRODUCTION TO AUTONOMOUS AI SYSTEMS

Title: Agentic AI: The Evolution of Autonomous AI Systems

Subtitle: From passive chatbots to persistent digital teammates.

Key points:
- Early AI waited for prompts and returned text in a single chat window.
- Modern autonomous AI systems can plan, use tools, operate computers,
  and run continuously in the background.
- Instead of asking AI how to do a task, users can delegate the outcome.

Opening question: “What changes when AI stops being a chat box you
prompt—and becomes a digital teammate with its own computer, memory,
and tools?”

SLIDE 2 — THE EVOLUTION OF AI SYSTEMS

Five generations of AI capability:

1.  Rule-Based & Predictive AI — Fixed logic and narrow classification
    or forecasting models.
2.  Generative Chatbots — Responds to one prompt at a time with text or
    code; the human coordinates every step.
3.  Tool-Using Assistants — Calls search, APIs, or calculators within a
    single turn, then stops and waits.
4.  Persistent Autonomous Agents — Operates across terminals, browsers,
    and messaging apps with long-term memory and 24/7 background tasks.
5.  Multi-Agent Ecosystems — Specialized digital teammates that delegate
    subtasks, share context, and collaborate toward complex goals.

Key takeaway: Each generation shifts more of the planning, execution,
and verification loop from the human to the system.

SLIDE 3 — ANATOMY OF AN AUTONOMOUS AI SYSTEM

Five core building blocks that make an AI system autonomous:

1.  Goal Decomposition & Planning — Breaks a high-level objective into
    ordered, verifiable steps.
2.  Persistent Memory & Reusable Skills — Remembers user preferences,
    past lessons, and repeatable procedures across sessions.
3.  Computer & Tool Use — Interacts directly with terminals, browsers,
    local files, graphical interfaces, and external APIs.
4.  Observation & Self-Correction — Inspects outputs, catches errors,
    and adjusts its plan without constant prompting.
5.  Guardrails & Human-in-the-Loop — Uses approval checkpoints,
    sandboxes, and secret redaction before high-impact actions.

Important: Autonomy is not just a bigger model—it is the harness of
memory, tools, feedback loops, and safety boundaries around the model.

SLIDE 4 — OPEN-SOURCE & LOCAL-FIRST AGENTS: HERMES AGENT & OPENCLAW

1.  Hermes Agent (Nous Research)
-   Open-source (MIT-licensed), model-agnostic agent framework.
-   Self-improving through skills: saves successful workflows as
    reusable SKILL.md procedures without retraining model weights.
-   Maintains cross-session memory across CLI, TUI, desktop, and chat
    gateways (Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Signal, Matrix, email).
-   Includes command approvals, containerized backends, and secret
    redaction.

2.  OpenClaw (OpenClaw Foundation)
-   Open-source, local-first autonomous agent and personal assistant
    running on the user’s own hardware for privacy and data control.
-   Lives inside everyday messaging apps (WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack,
    Discord, iMessage, Signal) instead of a separate dashboard.
-   Executes multi-step local file, email, calendar, and API tasks,
    with mobile companion triggers and approval workflows.

Key takeaway: Local-first and open-source agents bring persistent
autonomy directly to user-owned hardware and existing chat channels.

SLIDE 5 — CLOUD-NATIVE PERSONAL & TEAM AGENTS: MUSE & GROK BOT

1.  Muse (Meta)
-   Always-on cloud-backed personal and small-business agent powered by
    Meta models (such as Muse Spark).
-   Connects to everyday services: Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive,
    Notion, Messenger, OpenTable, and financial data via Plaid.
-   Handles scheduling conflicts, restaurant bookings, subscription
    audits, approved checkouts, and small-business social/ad workflows.

2.  Grok Bot (xAI / SpaceXSI)
-   Gives each bot a dedicated, persistent cloud computer with a real
    browser, file system, terminal, and app logins.
-   Runs 24/7 in the background even after the user closes their laptop.
-   “Teach-a-Task” learning: record a workflow once on screen and turn
    it into a repeatable routine.
-   Supports multiplayer teamwork inside Slack and Microsoft Teams with
    cross-bot delegation.

Key takeaway: Cloud-native agents trade local setup for always-on
managed environments—targeting either personal life or team operations.

SLIDE 6 — POPULAR GENERAL-PURPOSE & COMPUTER-USE AGENTS

1.  Manus
-   Bridges cloud sandboxes with local CLI and file-system execution.
-   Excels at heavy research, organizing large local folders, spinning
    up web apps, and running scheduled 24/7 background tasks.

2.  OpenAI Operator
-   Web-native browser and desktop task automation agent.
-   Specializes in navigating websites, filling out complex forms,
    bookings, shopping, and structured web data extraction.

3.  Claude Computer Use (Anthropic)
-   Uses a visual perception loop (screenshot -> inspect -> click/type/
    scroll) to operate software like a human.
-   Bridges legacy desktop software and mixed browser-desktop workflows
    that lack modern APIs.

Key takeaway: Modern agents span CLI-first execution, structured web
automation, and vision-driven GUI control.

SLIDE 7 — HOW AUTONOMOUS AGENTS WORK IN PRACTICE

The autonomous execution loop:

1.  Goal — Receive a high-level outcome via chat, CLI, or schedule.
2.  Plan — Break the objective into concrete sub-tasks and tool calls.
3.  Act — Execute commands via terminal, browser, API, or GUI clicks.
4.  Observe — Read outputs, screenshots, logs, or error messages.
5.  Evaluate & Adapt — Fix failures and retry until criteria are met.
6.  Remember — Persist context or save a reusable skill for next time.

Practical examples:
-   Personal life (Muse / OpenClaw): Audit recurring subscriptions,
    reschedule conflicting meetings, and confirm bookings with approval.
-   Team operations (Grok Bot / Manus): Monitor incoming reports,
    research competitors in a browser, update spreadsheets, and post a
    Slack summary.
-   Self-improving workflows (Hermes Agent): Complete a multi-step API
    migration once and store the steps as a reusable skill for future
    runs.

SLIDE 8 — MULTI-AGENT COLLABORATION: FROM SOLO BOTS TO DIGITAL TEAMS

What is a Multi-Agent System? Multiple autonomous agents with distinct
roles, tools, and permissions collaborating toward a shared objective.

Common agent roles in autonomous operations:
-   Coordinator / Chief of Staff: Interprets user intent, delegates
    work, and tracks completion.
-   Researcher: Browses the web, reads documents, and synthesizes facts.
-   Operator / Executor: Runs terminal commands, fills forms, or updates
    records across apps.
-   Reviewer / Guardrail: Verifies outputs, checks policy compliance,
    and requests human sign-off for sensitive actions.

Real-world patterns:
-   Grok Bot multiplayer teams delegating tasks across Slack/Teams.
-   Hermes Agent and OpenClaw routing specialized sub-tasks across
    local tools, containers, and messaging gateways.

SLIDE 9 — BENEFITS, RISKS, AND CONCLUSION

Benefits:
-   24/7 background execution without constant micromanagement.
-   Automates repetitive cross-app and browser busywork.
-   Compounding capability through persistent memory and saved skills.
-   Accessible through familiar chat apps instead of complex dashboards.

Risks & Challenges:
-   Prompt injection and untrusted web content hijacking agent actions.
-   Over-privileged local or cloud access (as highlighted in OpenClaw
    security and governance discussions).
-   Runaway API/compute costs from infinite loops or unverified retries.
-   Accountability and verification when agents act autonomously.

Conclusion: Autonomous AI systems mark the transition from software that
answers questions to software that reliably executes work on our behalf.

Closing statement: “The most effective users and teams won’t just write
better prompts—they will design clear goals, safe boundaries, and
reliable workflows for their autonomous agents.”

PRESENTATION APPROACH

Keep each slide focused on one main idea. Use short bullet points,
concise comparisons, and concrete workflow examples. Avoid dense
paragraphs, excessive jargon, and decorative visuals so the audience can
clearly grasp how autonomous AI systems evolved and operate today.
